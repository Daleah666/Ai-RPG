# World Spine — The Velvet Marches

## World name
**The Velvet Marches** (continent-spanning trade realm). Primary stage: the city-state of **Aurelline**, jewel of the southern coast.

## Tone
Decadent court fantasy with dark comedy edges. Perfume, silk, and public spectacle sit on top of contracts, blackmail, and body-as-currency politics. Mature without being only parody: desire is power, and power always invoices someone.

## Core tension (one sentence)
In Aurelline, beauty and desire are literal magic currencies—and those who trade away depth of mind for the **Ideal** become the realm’s most useful weapons.

## What “D&D” means here
Familiar bones (lineages, classes, gods, adventuring parties) run through a homebrew culture. Use 5e (or similar) stats as scaffolding; the setting’s unique pressure is **Glamour**—magic fueled by attraction, performance, and intentional emptiness.

## Map sketch (mental)
- **Aurelline** — stacked terraces of glass balconies, bathhouses, mirror-temples, and debt halls.
- **The Silk Road Spine** — caravan routes that move perfume, dyes, and “finished” Ideal vessels between cities.
- **The Pale Colleges** — inland academies that still treat intellect as holy; officially banned from Aurelline’s inner wards.
- **The Low Docks** — where failed Idealists, smugglers, and truth-sellers live under the glow of the upper city.
- **The Rosevault** — underground archive of True Names, broken contracts, and pre-Ideal identities.

## Cosmology (light)
- **The Bright Mirror** — a goddess of reflection, desire, and surface truth. She rewards those who become what others want to see.
- **The Hollow** — not a god but a pressure: the metaphysical space left when complexity is offered to Glamour. Priests call it sacred vacancy; skeptics call it predation.
- **Old Flame saints** — residual cults of craft, memory, and ugly honesty. Their miracles work poorly in Aurelline’s mirrored wards.

## Genre promises
Players/readers should expect: court intrigue, charm magic with costs, adult status games, identity erosion, and the question of whether performing emptiness is survival, strategy, or surrender.
