# Factions, Magic, and the Ideal

Four pillars only—what the central character constantly bumps into.

---

## 1. Power — Who rules Aurelline

**The Velvet Concord** is a council of perfume houses, pleasure-temples, and bonded lenders. Titles sound soft (**Patron**, **Mirror-Lord**, **Silk Elect**); enforcement is not.

What they want:
- Stable flow of Glamour through public desire
- Soft citizens who spend, obey spectacle, and do not organize around “ugly” truths
- Exportable Ideal vessels as diplomatic gifts and living advertisements

How beauty / charm / sex factor into status:
- Public attraction is taxable magic. Crowd heat at festivals fills city reservoirs.
- Sponsorship contracts bind Idealists to Patrons: body presentation, attendance, and “availability” are clause language.
- Sex is not illegal; **unlicensed intimacy that feeds private Glamour** without Concord tithe is.

Street truth: the Concord sells freedom as a fragrance and collects the bottle back.

---

## 2. Magic — Glamour

**Glamour** is enchantment drawn from being wanted, watched, or believed.

Sources:
- Attention (eyes on you)
- Desire directed at you (or performed toward another)
- Voluntary surrender of mental complexity into the Hollow

Costs:
- Memories thin first—names of books, reasons for anger, then reasons for love
- Spells that rely on Glamour weaken if you argue nuance in public
- Overdraw leaves **Bloom Burn**: fever, compulsive performance, loss of private face for days

Is “bimbo” curse, blessing, class, bloodline, or mask?
In Aurelline it is a **recognized vocation**: the **Idealist** (court slang: *bimbo*, *doll*, *finished rose*). Some are cursed into it, some buy in, some fake it. The Concord prefers volunteers—they sign cleaner contracts.

Mechanical-cultural rule of thumb:
- Higher Glamour potency ↔ lower tolerated complexity in public performance
- Private mind can remain sharper than the mask—until the Hollow takes a cut you did not budget for

---

## 3. Faction — The House of Finished Roses

The temple-guild that invents and enforces the Ideal.

- **Mirror-Abbess Calyx** — public face; sermons about sacred vacancy and beautiful service.
- **The Finishing Halls** — baths, fittings, voice coaches, and ritual emptying under rose-oil light.
- **The Ledger of Soft Names** — court names replacing birth names; True Names sealed for “protection.”
- **Handlers** — not priests, not lovers: career managers who schedule appearances and skim Glamour tithes.

House doctrine: an empty smile is a loaded wand. Complexity is a liability the city cannot afford in its icons.

---

## 4. Danger — Refusal and excess

Refuse the mold:
- Social death, revoked licenses, “ugliness taxes,” exile to the Low Docks
- Pale College sympathizers disappear after mirror-sermons name them
- Unfinished people are useful as cautionary entertainments

Lean into the Ideal too hard:
- The Hollow keeps what you offered; you may not reclaim it with apology
- Bloom Burn can make you perform for enemies as easily as patrons
- Some Idealists become **Open Vessels**—no private self left; Concord property in all but paperwork

Taboos:
- Speaking a sealed True Name in the upper wards
- Teaching literacy rituals to an Idealist under active contract
- Mixing Pale College memory-magic with House finishing rites (creates unstable “cracked mirrors”)

---

## The Idealist as world role (not punchline)

In this lore, a finished Idealist is primarily a **social weapon** and **magical vessel**:

- Spy / courtesan-diplomat who is underestimated on purpose
- Living battery for charm magic that grows as performed “depth” fades
- Status symbol for Patrons—and sometimes a voluntary power trade with eyes open

### Three hard rules

1. **Glamour only bites while you perform the Ideal.** Drop the mask in public and your enchantments gutter; keep the mask too long and the Hollow may treat the performance as the real you.
2. **True Names break the mask.** Speaking or hearing one’s sealed True Name in a mirrored ward can shatter Glamour mid-spell—and flood the Idealist with banned memory.
3. **Pleasure fuels, contracts meter.** Desire and intimate attention can recharge Glamour fast, but Concord law and House clauses decide who owns the surplus. Unmetered pleasure is smuggling.

Optional street proverb: *Soft head, sharp leash.*
