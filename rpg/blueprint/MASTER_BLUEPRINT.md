# Oops, I'm the Hero?! Master Blueprint

> Generated by `tools/build-blueprint.mjs` from the role documents in this folder and the data in `data/`. Edit those and rebuild; do not edit this file.

A tactical, high-stat, turn-based JRPG. Level cap 100. Six attributes on a 1–1,000 scale. HP and MP climb into the hundreds of thousands and, for bosses, the millions. Every chance is a d100 roll bounded to 5–95%. Stress is a second health bar that never levels up: 0–200, Resolve Check at 100.

Sections 1–5 are the work of the four specialist roles. Section 6 is the player character. Section 7 is the plug-in story system.

The arithmetic and rules in sections 1–3, 6 and 7 are implemented and tested in `engine/`. Sections 4 and 5 are data checked against those rules. There is no battle loop, skill resolver or status store yet: skill effects, statuses, a boss's phases and its telegraphed moves are written to be run by a game master, and `handoff/content-writer.md` and `handoff/boss-desk.md` list what a battle runner would need.

## 1. FOUNDATIONAL ENGINE MATH

This section is the arithmetic of one action: does it land, how hard, how much stress, who goes first. The reference implementation is `engine/combat.js`. Every number on this page was produced by running that code, and `test/combat.test.js` pins the tables, so a retune that makes this page wrong fails a test.

### 1.1 Ground rules

- **Effective attributes only.** Every formula reads an attribute after buffs (combined multiplier clamped to ×0.5–×1.5) and binds (×0.5, once), rounded down and clamped to 1–1000. "STR" below always means effective STR.
- **A and D.** A is the attacker's attribute named by the skill: STR or INT. D is the defender's answering attribute: CON against STR, WIL against INT. A skill that attacks with any other attribute is defended by CON if it is physical and WIL if it is magical.
- **Level** is the level of whoever is acting: the attacker for damage, the healer for healing.
- **One d100 per action.** Low is good. Nothing rolls twice to land.
- **Rounding.** Chances: round to the nearest whole percent, then clamp. Damage and healing: multiply everything first, round down once at the end. Stress: round to the nearest whole point. A half rounds up.

| Constant | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `HIT_BASE` | 75 | hit % when accuracy equals evasion |
| `HIT_SLOPE` | 40 | the most the accuracy ratio can add or remove (it never quite reaches 40) |
| `LCK_TO_ACC` | 0.5 | share of LCK added to AGI for accuracy and evasion |
| `CRIT_BASE` / `CRIT_SLOPE` | 5 / 20 | crit % at equal LCK / the most the LCK ratio can add or remove |
| `CRIT_FLOOR` / `CRIT_CAP` | 1 / 50 | the crit window |
| `CRIT_MULT` | 1.5 | damage multiplier on a crit |
| `FUMBLE_FROM` (constants.js) | 96 | first roll that fumbles |
| `DEF_WEIGHT` | 0.5 | what a point of defense is worth against a point of attack |
| `EVEN_PASS` | 0.5 | share of raw damage that gets through when A equals D |
| `GUARD_MULT` | 0.5 | damage multiplier against a guarding defender |
| `VARIANCE` | 0.90–1.10 | damage spread, in whole percents |
| `BASIC_POWER` | 2.0 | Power of the basic attack |
| `HEAL_SCALE` | 0.5 | healing per Power × Level × INT |
| `STRESS_K` | 600 | the WIL that shrugs off half of incoming stress |
| `STRESS_MIT_CAP` | 0.75 | upper limit on stress mitigation (a guard rail: WIL 1000 only reaches 0.625) |
| `INIT_SCALE` / `INIT_SPREAD` | 10 / 0.2 | initiative per point of AGI / size of the random part |

### 1.2 The d100 Bounded Percentile Hit Chance

```
ACC    = attacker AGI + attacker LCK ÷ 2
EVA    = defender AGI + defender LCK ÷ 2

Hit %  = clamp( 75 + 40 × (ACC − EVA) ÷ (ACC + EVA) + skill.acc + hitBonus , 5 , 95 )

Crit % = clamp( 5 + 20 × (LCKa − LCKd) ÷ (LCKa + LCKd) + skill.crit + critBonus , 1 , 50 )
         and never more than Hit %
```

Roll one d100 and read it in this order. The first line that matches is the result.

1. **Fumble**: the roll is 96 or more. Always a failure, with a consequence chosen by whoever called the roll.
2. **Crit**: the roll is at or under Crit %. Damage ×1.5.
3. **Hit**: the roll is at or under Hit %.
4. **Miss**: anything else.

In an even match that is 1–5 crit, 6–75 hit, 76–95 miss, 96–100 fumble.

Things that follow from the formula:

- Level is not in it. A level 1 duel and a level 100 duel between evenly matched fighters roll against the same 75.
- With no skill modifier, Hit % can only run from 35 (ACC 1.5 against EVA 1500, the worst mismatch the attribute range allows) to 95. The 5% floor is only reached through `skill.acc` or `hitBonus` penalties.
- With no skill modifier, Crit % runs from 1 to 25. The cap of 50 is only reached through `skill.crit` and `critBonus`.
- At the 95 cap there are no plain misses left: 96–100 is the fumble band.
- Crit is a band inside the hit roll, not a chance of its own, which is why its window is 1–50 instead of 5–95.

**Band edges an ability may move.** These are the only handles. None of them adds a roll.

| Option | Effect |
|---|---|
| `hitBonus` | flat points added before the clamp. Use a negative number for a defender's dodge effect. |
| `hitFloor`, `hitCap` | replace the 5 and the 95. If they cross, the floor wins. |
| `critBonus` | flat points added to Crit % before its clamp. |
| `fumbleFrom` | first roll that fumbles. Lower widens the band and eats the top of the hit range. 101 switches fumbles off. A roll of 1 can never fumble. |
| `damageMult` | multiplies the damage of a landed hit. |

**Why this shape.** The swing is driven by a ratio, so AGI 20 against 10 at level 1 means exactly what AGI 800 against 400 means at level 100, and it is bounded, so no lead ever makes a roll automatic. The base is 75 because missing one swing in four keeps positioning and accuracy skills worth choosing without making turns feel wasted. LCK counts at half weight so AGI stays the accuracy attribute and luck is seasoning. The fumble band is a fixed five numbers at the top of the die: the finest duelist alive still trips over the rug one swing in twenty, which is the slapstick engine and the reason the cap is 95.

### 1.3 The Damage Formula

```
Damage = floor( Power × Level × A × Pass × variance × crit × affinity × guard × damageMult )

Pass   = 1.5 × A ÷ (2 × A + D)
```

In one line: `Power × Level × 1.5 × A² ÷ (2A + D)`, then the multipliers.

| Term | Value |
|---|---|
| Power | from the skill. Basic attack 2.0, skills about 3–12, ultimates 20 and up. |
| Level | the attacker's level, 1–100. |
| A, D | effective attacking and defending attributes (section 1.1). |
| Pass | the share that gets past the defender's attribute. Exactly 0.5 when A equals D, 0.75 at most. |
| variance | 0.90 to 1.10 in steps of 0.01, every step equally likely. Drawn after the attack roll, and only if it landed. It spreads damage; it cannot turn a hit into a miss. |
| crit | ×1.5 on a crit, otherwise ×1. |
| affinity | weak ×1.5, normal ×1, resist ×0.5, null ×0, absorb ×1 as healing (see below). |
| guard | ×0.5 if the defender is guarding, otherwise ×1. |
| damageMult | ×1 unless a passive says otherwise. |

**Order of operations.**

1. Take effective A and D.
2. Multiply every term as real numbers. Do not round along the way.
3. Round down once.
4. If the hit landed and was not nulled, the result is at least 1.
5. **Null**: the result is 0. **Absorb**: compute the hit as if the affinity were normal (crit and guard still apply) and add that amount to the defender's HP instead of removing it. `damage()` returns the positive amount and `resolveAttack()` sets `absorbed: true`; the caller does the healing.

A skill with Power 0 deals 0: buffs and pure status skills do not chip.

**The shortcut worth memorising.** At A = D, Pass is one half, so a basic attack does exactly **Level × A**. Every point of Power is 5% of the HP of a peer whose CON equals your attacking attribute: Power 2 is a tenth of their bar, Power 10 is half, Power 20 is all of it.

Attack moves damage much more than defense does, because A appears twice. From an even match, a ×1.5 attack buff multiplies damage by 1.69 and a ×0.5 attack debuff (or bound arms) by 0.38. A ×1.5 defense buff multiplies damage taken by 0.86 and a ×0.5 defense debuff by 1.20. Guarding (×0.5) is the strong defensive tool; defense buffs are the slow one.

**Worked example, level 1.** Valerie (level 1, STR 14) swings at a Puddle Slime (CON 10) with a basic attack, Power 2. Plain hit, variance 0.96.

```
Pass   = 1.5 × 14 ÷ (2 × 14 + 10) = 21 ÷ 38       = 0.5526
Raw    = 2 × 1 × 14 × 0.5526                      = 15.47
Damage = floor( 15.47 × 0.96 ) = floor( 14.85 )   = 14
```

The slime has 10 × 1 × 10 = 100 HP. At variance 1.00 the same hit does 15, so it takes seven of them.

**Worked example, level 50.** A mage (level 50, INT 403) casts a Power 6 fire spell at a target with WIL 302 that is weak to fire. The roll is a crit. Variance 1.07.

```
Pass   = 1.5 × 403 ÷ (2 × 403 + 302) = 604.5 ÷ 1108   = 0.5456
Raw    = 6 × 50 × 403 × 0.5456                        = 65,960.33
       × 1.07 variance                                = 70,577.56
       × 1.5 crit                                     = 105,866.34
       × 1.5 weak                                     = 158,799.50
Damage = floor( 158,799.50 )                          = 158,799
```

A trained target at that level (CON 302) has 151,000 HP. Crit on a weakness is ×2.25, and with a Power 6 spell that is the whole bar.

**Worked example, level 100.** A warrior (level 100, STR 800, under a ×1.25 STR buff, so effective STR 1000) lands a Power 20 ultimate on a boss with CON 600. The boss is guarding and resists physical. Plain hit, variance 0.94.

```
Pass   = 1.5 × 1000 ÷ (2 × 1000 + 600) = 1500 ÷ 2600   = 0.5769
Raw    = 20 × 100 × 1000 × 0.5769                      = 1,153,846.15
       × 0.94 variance                                 = 1,084,615.38
       × 0.5 resist                                    = 542,307.69
       × 0.5 guard                                     = 271,153.85
Damage = floor( 271,153.85 )                           = 271,153
```

A boss with CON 600 at level 100 has 10 × 100 × 600 × 20 = 12,000,000 HP, so this is 2.3% of the bar. Without the guard and the resistance, at variance 1.00, the same hit does 1,153,846.

**Why this shape.** `Level × A` grows on the same Level × attribute square as max HP (`10 × Level × CON`), so a fight lasts as long at level 100 as at level 1 while the numbers go from tens to hundreds of thousands. Pass is a ratio, so only relative attributes matter, more defense always helps but with diminishing returns, and damage can never reach zero or go negative: there is no armor wall to make a fight unwinnable. Defense carries half the weight of attack because CON already buys HP. The FOUNDATION baseline, `A² ÷ (A + D)`, counted CON at full weight twice, and a defender with CON at 0.6× the attacker's STR died in 4.8 hits, outside the 6–14 anchor at every level. `1.5 × A ÷ (2A + D)` is the same curve with defense at half weight, rescaled so an even match still passes exactly half: nothing changes at A = D, and the soft end of the band moves from 4.8 hits to 5.2.

An archetype whose CON ÷ STR sits outside that range breaks the anchor in a mirror duel.

Fewer than 1% of those duels ended one hit outside the ranges shown.

At even AGI and LCK, 75 swings in 100 land, so count about four swings for every three landed hits: an even duel is 10 landed hits and 13 to 14 swings.

### 1.5 Healing

```
Heal = floor( Power × Level × INT × 0.5 × variance × crit × mult ), at least 1
```

INT is the healer's effective INT (a skill may name another attribute). Variance, crit (×1.5) and `mult` all default to ×1; heals do not roll to hit.

**How it keeps pace with HP.** Healing grows with `Level × INT` just as max HP grows with `Level × CON`, so as a share of the target's bar a heal is `5% × Power × INT ÷ target CON` at every level. The 0.5 is the same one half that an even hit passes, so a heal of Power P undoes one even-stat hit of Power P.

| Healer (signature INT) | Power 3 heals | Power 8 heals | Share of an ally with CON equal to that INT |
|---|---|---|---|
| Level 1, INT 14 | 21 | 56 | 15% / 40% of 140 |
| Level 50, INT 403 | 30,225 | 80,600 | 15% / 40% of 201,500 |
| Level 100, INT 800 | 120,000 | 320,000 | 15% / 40% of 800,000 |

A squishier ally gets a bigger share from the same heal: the level 100 Power 3 heal is 20% of a CON 600 ally and 40% of a CON 300 one. A bound head halves INT and therefore the heal.

### 1.6 The Stress Mitigation formula

```
Mitigation   = min( 0.75 , WIL ÷ (WIL + 600) )
Stress taken = max( 1 , round( Base × mult × (1 − Mitigation) ) )
```

WIL is the target's effective WIL, so a bound head (WIL halved) takes more. `ignoreWill` skips the mitigation. `mult` scales stress gains and defaults to 1. A positive base always lands at least 1. A negative base is stress relief and passes straight through: no mitigation, no `mult`. The result is the amount to add; clamping the bar to 0–200 is the caller's job.

| Base stress | WIL 1 | WIL 100 | WIL 250 | WIL 500 | WIL 750 | WIL 1000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| share taken | 99.8% | 85.7% | 70.6% | 54.5% | 44.4% | 37.5% |
| 10 | 10 | 9 | 7 | 5 | 4 | 4 |
| 25 | 25 | 21 | 18 | 14 | 11 | 9 |
| 50 | 50 | 43 | 35 | 27 | 22 | 19 |

**Why this shape.** The stress bar is the one bar that never levels up, so its defense must flatten out instead of scaling. `WIL ÷ (WIL + 600)` gives a WIL 1 farmhand no help at all, takes half off at WIL 600 and stops at 62.5% off at the WIL 1000 cap: willpower is always worth having and never becomes immunity. Because WIL rises with level, the same base stress lands softer late in the game, so content has to raise base stress over the campaign to keep the same bite (a base of 24 on a trained level 100 hero lands as 12, the same as a base of 12 at level 1).

### 1.7 Turn order

```
Initiative = AGI × 10 + a whole number drawn evenly from 0 to AGI × 2
```

Higher acts first. The random part is at most 20% of the base, so AGI decides the order and the dice only shuffle near-ties. Ties go to the higher effective LCK, then to heroes before enemies.

| AGI lead | 0% | 5% | 10% | 15% | 20% or more |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Faster one acts first | 50% | 73% | 88% | 97% | always |

Bound legs halve AGI: a bound fighter acts after everyone whose AGI is more than 60% of the bound fighter's unbound AGI.

### 1.8 At the table: quick reference

Four lookups resolve an attack and a stress hit by hand. Use the nearest row. If the game master can run code, call `resolveAttack` and `stressTaken` instead and use these tables to explain the result.

**Step 1. To hit.** ACC and EVA are AGI plus half of LCK. Find ACC ÷ EVA, then add the skill's accuracy.

| ACC ÷ EVA | ¼ | ⅓ | ½ | ⅔ | ¾ | even | 1⅓ | 1½ | 2 | 3 or more |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hit % | 51 | 55 | 62 | 67 | 69 | 75 | 81 | 83 | 88 | 95 |

Never below 5, never above 95.

**Step 2. To crit.** Attacker LCK ÷ defender LCK, then add the skill's crit.

| LCK ratio | ½ or less | ¾ | even | 1⅓ | 1½ | 2 | 3 | 4 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Crit % | 1 | 2 | 5 | 8 | 9 | 12 | 15 | 17 | 21 |

Never below 1, never above 50, never above the hit chance.

**Step 3. Roll d100.** 96 or more: fumble. At or under Crit %: crit. At or under Hit %: hit. Otherwise: miss.

**Step 4. Damage.** `Level × A × (Power ÷ 2) × defense factor`. A basic attack on an even target is just Level × A.

| D ÷ A | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | even | 1¼ | 1½ | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Defense factor | ×1.33 | ×1.20 | ×1.09 | ×1.00 | ×0.92 | ×0.86 | ×0.75 | ×0.60 | ×0.50 | ×0.43 | ×0.25 |

Then: crit ×1.5, weak ×1.5, resist ×0.5, guarding ×0.5, null = nothing, absorb = heals that much. Any hit that lands and is not nulled does at least 1. Skip variance at the table, or nudge the number up or down by a tenth for flavour.

**One attack, start to finish.** A level 60 knight (STR 480, AGI 300, LCK 300) uses a Power 4 skill with accuracy −10 on an ogre (CON 720, AGI 200, LCK 200).

- To hit: ACC 450 against EVA 300 is 1½, so 83, minus 10 is **73**. Luck 300 against 200 is 1½, so crit **9**.
- The d100 comes up 41: a hit.
- Damage: D ÷ A is 720 ÷ 480 = 1½, factor ×0.86. 60 × 480 × 2 × 0.86 = **49,536**. The engine's exact figure is 49,371.
- The ogre roars back for base stress 20. The knight's WIL is 300: **13** stress.

## 2. LEVEL 1 - 100 PROGRESSION & STAT MATRIX

This section fixes four things: how characters level, what their attributes are at each level, how large the HP and MP bars grow, and what enemies pay.

The code is `engine/progression.js`. The data is `data/archetypes.json`. `test/progression.test.js` pins every rule below. Every table is printed output from that code.

### 2.1 The system on one card

| Quantity | Rule |
|---|---|
| EXP from level L to L + 1 | `50 × L × (L + 15)` |
| EXP for one standard enemy | `50 × enemy level`, paid in full to every party member |
| Gold for one standard enemy | `10 × enemy level`, paid once to the party |
| Rank multiplier on EXP and gold | minion ×0.4, standard ×1, elite ×4, boss ×30, superboss ×100 |
| EXP from an enemy below your level | yield `÷ (1 + (gap ÷ 10)²)`, rounded down, minimum 1 |
| Attribute at level L | `L1 + (L100 − L1) × (L − 1) ÷ 99`, rounded |
| Max HP | `10 × Level × CON`, times the enemy rank multiplier |
| Max MP | `Level × WIL` |
| Reference fight | four standard enemies of the party's level |
| Reference fights to gain a level | `(L + 15) ÷ 4` |
| Gold earned across level L | `10 × L × (L + 15)` |

A game master needs this card and the archetype tables in 2.4. Nothing else in the section is required at the table.

### 2.2 The EXP curve

```
expToNext(L) = 50 × L² + 750 × L + 0  =  50 × L × (L + 15)      for L = 1..99
expToNext(100) = 0
```

The coefficients are `EXP_CURVE = { QUAD: 50, LIN: 750, CONST: 0 }`. Level 1 to 2 costs 800 EXP. Level 1 to 100 costs 20,130,000 EXP. Both sit inside anchor A8.

| Level | EXP to next | Total EXP to reach |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 800 | 0 |
| 5 | 5,000 | 9,000 |
| 10 | 12,500 | 48,000 |
| 20 | 35,000 | 266,000 |
| 30 | 67,500 | 754,000 |
| 40 | 110,000 | 1,612,000 |
| 50 | 162,500 | 2,940,000 |
| 60 | 225,000 | 4,838,000 |
| 70 | 297,500 | 7,406,000 |
| 80 | 380,000 | 10,744,000 |
| 90 | 472,500 | 14,952,000 |
| 99 | 564,300 | 19,565,700 |

`totalExpForLevel(level)` returns the right-hand column. `levelForExp(totalExp)` reverses it: 1,000,000 EXP is level 33.

**Why this shape.** A kill pays in proportion to the enemy's level. A level costs in proportion to the square of yours. Divide one by the other and the fights needed per level climb in a straight line. The `+ 15` sets the floor: without it, level 1 would take a quarter of a fight. The 50 sets the total at 20 million.

### 2.3 Fights to level

The reference fight is four standard enemies of the party's level. All pacing in this document is measured in reference fights.

EXP is not split. Each party member receives the full yield of every enemy, knocked-out members included. A party that travels together shares one EXP total and one level.

| Level | EXP per standard enemy | EXP per reference fight | Fights to level | Fights so far |

**Why this shape.** Without the gap rule, an enemy at half your level has a quarter of the HP and pays half the EXP. Farming it would beat fighting peers. The multiplier stays above 90% for the first three levels, so a party slightly ahead of its zone keeps most of its EXP. It never reaches zero, so no level exists where a kill suddenly stops counting.

### 2.5 Bar scaling

### 2.7 Custom archetypes

`data/archetypes.json` has this shape. Add an entry, or pass an object of the same shape straight to the functions.

```json
{
  "id": "warrior", "name": "Warrior",
  "identity": "One line.", "want": "What they are after.", "cost": "What it costs them.",
  "signature": "CON",
  "l1":       { "STR": 15,  "INT": 8,   "AGI": 10,  "CON": 16,  "WIL": 9,   "LCK": 12 },
  "l100":     { "STR": 760, "INT": 300, "AGI": 520, "CON": 800, "WIL": 300, "LCK": 620 },
  "exponent": { "STR": 1,   "INT": 1,   "AGI": 1,   "CON": 1,   "WIL": 1,   "LCK": 1 }
}
```

`validateArchetype(archetype)` returns `{ ok, errors, warnings }`. It reports every fault in one pass and names the archetype, the attribute and the rule. It checks:

1. `id` is lowercase snake case. `name`, `identity`, `want` and `cost` are non-empty strings.
2. `signature` is one of the six attribute keys.
3. `l1` and `l100` hold all six attributes as integers and no other keys.
4. The budget rules at the top of 2.4.
5. Each exponent is a number from 0.5 to 2. A missing exponent counts as 1.
6. No attribute drops between any two levels.

**Exponents.** `growthCurve(l1, l100, level, exponent)` raises the progress from level 1 to level 100 to the power of the exponent. Below 1, the attribute blooms early. Above 1, it blooms late. Both ends stay exact.

**The warning.** If the six-attribute total strays more than 5% from the budget line at any level, the validator adds a warning and names the worst level. The archetype still passes. An archetype with every exponent at 0.5 carries 40% more attributes than its peers at level 50, so pair each early bloomer with a late one.

## 3. PSYCHOLOGICAL & STATUS SYSTEMS

This section is the two systems that stop a fight from being a damage race: **stress** (the second health bar, the Resolve Check, Afflictions, Virtues, permanent marks and the Breakdown) and **body-part binds**. The reference implementations are `engine/stress.js` and `engine/binds.js`; the tables are `data/afflictions.json` and `data/virtues.json`. Every number on this page was produced by running that code, and `test/stress.test.js` and `test/binds.test.js` pin the rules, so a retune that makes this page wrong fails a test.

### 3.1 Ground rules

- **One d100 answers one question.** The Resolve Check, the turn check and a bind attempt are one roll each, read against bands. Nothing here rolls twice. When a rule says a target or a skill is "read off the dice", it is picked with the roll already on the table: take the roll modulo the length of the list (`pickByRoll`). It is the same trick as the hero's `befuddledPart`.
- **Low is good. 96–100 is bad news. Doubles are for keeps.** On the Resolve Check low rolls are Virtues. On the turn check and on a bind attempt 96–100 is the fumble band, as it is everywhere else. Doubles (11, 22 … 99, and 100 read as 00) make a Resolve result permanent.
- **The world's stress goes through Willpower. The party's does not.** Stress from enemies, traps and events is mitigated by effective WIL (section 1.6). Stress that party members hand each other, and all stress relief, is flat: the number written is the number the bar moves. A flat number means the same thing at level 1 and level 100, and it matches the card text exactly.
- **Bounded.** Stress is 0–200 at every level. The Virtue chance runs 10–40. The turn check and the bind chance are clamped to 5–95 like every other roll.

### 3.2 The stress bar

Every hero has a stress bar from 0 to 200. It is the same bar at level 1 and at level 100.

| Stress | Name | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0–49 | Calm | Nothing. An Affliction ends the moment relief brings its owner down into this stretch. |
| 50–99 | Strained | Nothing yet. |
| reaching 100 | **Resolve Check** | One d100 decides between a Virtue and an Affliction (3.4). |
| 100–199 | Cracking | Whatever the check gave them is running. An Affliction acts more often the higher the bar climbs. |
| reaching 200 | **Breakdown** | Someone has to pay, and the player chooses who (3.8). |

- Stress is a whole number, clamped to 0–200.
- **One check per crossing.** The Resolve Check fires when stress reaches 100 from below and the character has no Affliction or Virtue. A character who already has one makes no new check however often the bar crosses 100. A character whose state is cleared while they sit at 100 or more makes no check either: they have to drop under 100 and reach it again.
- One hit that carries the bar through 100 and on to 200 is a Breakdown, not a check.
- A knocked-out character takes no stress and makes no checks. Neither does one who has Stormed Off.

**The choice.** Relief costs a turn, MP, an item or gold (3.9). Every point you remove is an action that was not damage or healing. Every point you leave is a step toward a roll you lose three times in four.

### 3.3 Where stress comes from

Stress from the world is written as a **tier**, and `stressBase(tier, level)` turns the tier into the base number for a skill or an event:

```
Base = round( Lands × (1 + benchmark attribute at that level ÷ 600) )      Lands: weak 5, medium 12, severe 24
```

The base rises with level exactly as fast as a trained Willpower learns to shrug it off, so a hit of each tier lands 5, 12 or 24 on a character whose WIL sits on the benchmark line at every level. The bar is 100 points to the Resolve Check: about eight medium hits, or four severe ones. What moves with level is the gap between the classes.

| Level | Weak | Medium | Severe | A Medium hit lands on Warrior / Mage / Rogue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5 | 12 | 24 | 12 / 12 / 12 |
| 10 | 6 | 13 | 27 | 12 / 11 / 12 |
| 25 | 6 | 15 | 30 | 13 / 11 / 13 |
| 50 | 8 | 18 | 36 | 14 / 11 / 13 |
| 75 | 9 | 21 | 42 | 15 / 10 / 14 |
| 100 | 10 | 24 | 48 | 16 / 10 / 14 |

At level 1 everyone takes the same. By level 100 the same howl costs the Warrior 16 and the Mage 10: stress finds the Warrior first, as section 2 intends. Flat stress written on a move (bosses do this) skips the tier lookup and goes straight to WIL.

| embarrassment | ×1.5 on Valerie unless she is a Total Airhead. |
| wound | Martyr Complex takes ×0.5. |
| death | Someone going down: the standard sources that land hardest. Passives key on it (section 4). |

**Stress the party gives itself is flat.** It ignores WIL and is tagged mental: 5–10 to every other member when an ally's Affliction starts, 3–8 when an Affliction acts out, 20 from a Breakdown that is Had Out. No amount of Willpower helps you watch a friend fall apart.

### 3.4 The Resolve Check

When stress reaches 100, roll **one d100**. That roll is the whole check.

1. **The band the roll falls in picks the side and the entry.** At the base odds 1–25 is a Virtue and 26–100 is an Affliction: 25% and 75%, exactly.
2. **An even roll is personal.** If the character has an entry of their own for the side rolled, it replaces the table entry. On an odd roll the table stands.
3. **Doubles are permanent.** 11, 22, 33, 44, 55, 66, 77, 88, 99 or 100: the result leaves a mark when it ends (3.7). Any other roll is temporary and leaves nothing.

| d100 | Result | Side | In one line | Party | Doubles in the band |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1–5 | **On a Roll** | Virtue | To do the big brave thing while everyone is watching. | sheds 8 | none |
| 6–10 | **Unflappable** | Virtue | To be the calm one in the room. | sheds 10 | none |
| 11–15 | **In the Zone** | Virtue | To finish the one thing in front of them. | sheds 5 | 11 |
| 16–20 | **Mother Hen** | Virtue | To get everyone home in one piece. | sheds 12 | none |
| 21–25 | **Second Wind** | Virtue | To prove they can take it. | sheds 6 | 22 |
| 26–36 | **Martyr Complex** | Affliction | Takes hits nobody asked them to take, then turns the healing down. | takes 5 | 33 |
| 37–47 | **Spooked** | Affliction | Hides at the moment the party needs a hit landed. | takes 6 | 44 |
| 48–58 | **Loot Goblin** | Affliction | Spends the party's supplies on themselves and their turns on nobody else. | takes 5 | 55 |
| 59–69 | **Tinfoil Helm** | Affliction | Turns help down at the exact moment it is needed. | takes 8 | 66 |
| 70–80 | **Backseat Hero** | Affliction | Wears the party down faster than the monsters do. | takes 8 | 77 |
| 81–90 | **Doomsayer** | Affliction | Stops trying, and makes stopping sound sensible. | takes 10 | 88 |
| 91–100 | **Lost the Plot** | Affliction | Nobody, including them, knows what their turn will be. | takes 8 | 99, 100 |

"Party" is what every other party member's bar does when the result starts. Each Affliction's band holds exactly one doubles roll at the base odds (Lost the Plot holds two), so every Scar can happen. Of the hundred faces, 8 leave a Scar and 2 leave a Silver Lining.

**Shifting the odds.** Abilities and circumstances add to or take from the Virtue chance. The total shift is clamped to ±15, so the Virtue chance runs from 10 to 40.

| Source | Shift |
|---|---|
| Each ally currently in a Virtue | +5 |
| Each ally currently Afflicted | −5 |
| An Unflappable ally | +5 more, so +10 in all |
| A Doomsayer ally | −5 more, so −10 in all |
| Valerie standing and Ditzy / a Total Airhead (allies' checks only) | +5 / +10 |
| A skill, item or event | whatever it says |

Knocked-out allies and allies who Stormed Off count for nothing. `partyResolveShift(allies, extra)` adds this up.

**How the bands move.** A shift moves the line between the two sides. Each side keeps its entries in order and stretches or squeezes them evenly:

```
upper edge = start of the side + round( base width up to and including this entry × new width of the side ÷ base width of the side )

Virtues:      start 0,               base width 25, new width = Virtue chance
Afflictions:  start = Virtue chance, base width 75, new width = 100 − Virtue chance
```

A half rounds up, and the last entry on each side always ends where its side ends, so the bands fill 1–100 with no gap and no overlap at every shift. At +10: Mother Hen ends at round(20 × 35 ÷ 25) = 28, and Spooked ends at 35 + round(22 × 65 ÷ 75) = 35 + 19 = 54. Nobody needs to do this at the table: `resolveBands(tables, shift)` returns the layout, and here it is for every shift a table will see. For a shift in between, use the nearer column.

| Result | −15 | −10 | −5 | **no shift** | +5 | +10 | +15 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *Virtue chance* | *10%* | *15%* | *20%* | *25%* | *30%* | *35%* | *40%* |
| On a Roll | 1–2 | 1–3 | 1–4 | **1–5** | 1–6 | 1–7 | 1–8 |
| Unflappable | 3–4 | 4–6 | 5–8 | **6–10** | 7–12 | 8–14 | 9–16 |
| In the Zone | 5–6 | 7–9 | 9–12 | **11–15** | 13–18 | 15–21 | 17–24 |
| Mother Hen | 7–8 | 10–12 | 13–16 | **16–20** | 19–24 | 22–28 | 25–32 |
| Second Wind | 9–10 | 13–15 | 17–20 | **21–25** | 25–30 | 29–35 | 33–40 |
| Martyr Complex | 11–23 | 16–27 | 21–32 | **26–36** | 31–40 | 36–45 | 41–49 |
| Spooked | 24–36 | 28–40 | 33–43 | **37–47** | 41–51 | 46–54 | 50–58 |
| Loot Goblin | 37–50 | 41–52 | 44–55 | **48–58** | 52–61 | 55–64 | 59–66 |
| Tinfoil Helm | 51–63 | 53–65 | 56–67 | **59–69** | 62–71 | 65–73 | 67–75 |
| Backseat Hero | 64–76 | 66–77 | 68–79 | **70–80** | 72–81 | 74–83 | 76–84 |
| Doomsayer | 77–88 | 78–89 | 80–89 | **81–90** | 82–91 | 84–91 | 85–92 |
| Lost the Plot | 89–100 | 90–100 | 90–100 | **91–100** | 92–100 | 92–100 | 93–100 |
| *Rolls that leave a Scar* | 10 | 9 | 9 | 8 | 8 | 7 | 7 |
| *Rolls that leave a Silver Lining* | 0 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 3 |

A shift moves the doubles too. Crush the Virtue chance to 10 and no roll leaves a Silver Lining at all.

**What the check does to the party.** Averaged over the hundred faces, one Resolve Check at the base odds costs every other party member 3.3 stress. At +10 it costs 1.7, at +15 1.0, and at −15 it costs 5.5. An Affliction also takes 5 off the next person's Virtue chance. Checks come in chains, and the first one sets the direction.

**Personal entries.** A character can carry one Affliction and one Virtue of their own, passed to `resolveCheck` as `personal: { affliction, virtue }`. Half the die is even, so half that character's results are their own. A personal entry has the same shape as a table entry without `range`; only `id`, `name` and `text` are required. One that names no mark of its own leaves Frayed Nerves or the Silver Lining on doubles. Valerie's two are in section 6.

**Worked example.** A level 50 Warrior (WIL 153) is on 92 stress when a medium horror hit arrives: base 18, lands 14, bar 106. The bar reached 100 from below: Resolve Check. Valerie is a Total Airhead (+10) and the Mage is already Spooked (−5): shift +5, Virtue on 1–30. The roll is 66. In the +5 column 62–71 is **Tinfoil Helm**. The roll is even, but the Warrior has no personal Affliction, so the table stands. The roll is doubles: when this ends it leaves the **Trust Issues** Scar. Right now the Warrior's Willpower drops to 122, every other party member takes 8 stress, and from the Warrior's next turn the Affliction acts 30% of the time. A 27 would have been Second Wind: with no shift it is Martyr Complex.

**The choice.** Spend relief to stay under 100, or let someone ride it. One roll in four is a Virtue, the first result bends the odds for everyone after it, and you decide who goes first by deciding whom you calm down.

### 3.5 Afflictions

An Affliction is a personality that takes over. It changes what the character will and will not do.

- **When it starts** its attribute multipliers apply at once, as ordinary modifiers under the ×0.5–×1.5 clamp of section 1.1, and every other party member takes the stress listed, flat. The owner's bar does not move.
- **At the start of each of their turns** they make the turn check below.
- **While it lasts** every other party member's Virtue chance is 5 lower.
- **Outside battle** there are no turn checks. Its attribute changes, its standing refusals and its stress multipliers stay, and the game master plays its want and its cost.
- **It ends** when one of these happens: relief takes the owner under 50 stress; a Night Off in town; a snap-out cure such as Smelling Salts; a Breakdown. Winning the battle does not end it, and neither does a night at an inn unless the inn's relief gets them under 50.

**The turn check.** One d100 at the start of an afflicted character's turn. The Affliction's chance to act is its base chance plus 5 for every full 20 stress over 100, clamped to 5–95.

- **Composed**: the roll is at or under 100 minus the chance. The player has the turn.
- **Acts out**: the roll is above that. The Affliction's act happens. Some acts take the turn; some happen and then the player acts.
- **Meltdown**: the roll is 96–100. The Affliction's worst act happens instead.

Whatever the act says they refuse, they refuse until their next turn check. "The most hurt ally" always means the ally with the lowest HP as a share of their maximum. A Tinfoil Helm on 130 stress acts 35% of the time: 1–65 composed, 66–95 acts out, 96–100 meltdown.

| Stress | Base 30%: Martyr Complex, Loot Goblin, Tinfoil Helm, Backseat Hero | Base 35%: Spooked, Doomsayer | Base 40%: Lost the Plot |
|---|---|---|---|
| under 100 | 30% | 35% | 40% |
| 100–119 | 30% | 35% | 40% |
| 120–139 | 35% | 40% | 45% |
| 140–159 | 40% | 45% | 50% |
| 160–179 | 45% | 50% | 55% |
| 180–199 | 50% | 55% | 60% |

### 3.6 Virtues

A Virtue is also a personality, and it also has a cost.

- **When it starts** the owner sheds 40 or 50 of their own stress, every other party member sheds 5–12, and its attribute multipliers apply.
- **At the start of each of their turns** they make the turn check: on a roll of 30 or less the Virtue's **flourish** happens, free, and then the player acts.
- **While it lasts** every other party member's Virtue chance is 5 higher, and the owner makes no new Resolve Check even if their bar climbs back over 100.
- **It ends** when the battle ends (one rolled outside battle lasts through the next battle), or in a Breakdown. Calming down does not end it.

### 3.7 Permanent marks: Scars and the Silver Lining

A Resolve Check rolled on doubles is permanent. The result itself is still temporary; what stays is its mark, which goes onto the character sheet when the Affliction or Virtue ends.

**Scars.** An Affliction rolled on doubles leaves its Scar: the same personality, smaller, for good.

| Scar | Left by | Rolled on | What it does, for good |
|---|---|---|---|
| **No, No, I'm Fine** | Martyr Complex | 33 | The first heal an ally aims at them in each battle is waved off and wasted. |
| **Flinch** | Spooked | 44 | Takes 25% more stress from horror, for good. |
| **Sticky Fingers** | Loot Goblin | 55 | The party's gold from every battle they fight in is 5% lighter. |
| **Trust Issues** | Tinfoil Helm | 66 | The first buff an ally aims at them in each battle is refused and wasted. |
| **Running Commentary** | Backseat Hero | 77 | Any ally who fumbles while this character is in the fight takes 3 extra stress. |
| **Glass Half Empty** | Doomsayer | 88 | Stress relief works 20% less well on them, for good. |
| **Loose Screw** | Lost the Plot | 99, 100 | Their own fumble band is one wider: they fumble on 95 or higher, for good. |
| **Frayed Nerves** | a personal Affliction with no Scar of its own; Bottling a Breakdown | n/a | Takes 10% more stress from everything, for good. |

- A Scar the character already carries is not gained twice.
- A character carries at most 3 Scars. A fourth pushes one out, and the player picks which (`drop`); with no pick the oldest goes.
- **Talk It Out**, in town, removes one Scar for the price of a major purchase (3.9).

**The Silver Lining.** Every Virtue rolled on doubles (11 or 22 at the base odds) leaves the same mark, and a character holds one at a time. Spend it to erase one Scar, or keep it: it cancels the next Scar they would gain. When it cancels a Scar, both are gone.

**The choice.** A Scar costs a weapon's worth of gold to remove: buy the sword, or get the fighter's nerve back. A Silver Lining can be spent on the Scar you have or kept against the one you fear.

### 3.8 Breakdown

At 200 stress the character has a Breakdown. It ends whatever Affliction or Virtue they were in, it is never a game over, and nobody is hurt or lost for good. The bill is real and the player picks who gets it.

| Choice | Who pays | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Storm Off** | The fight | They walk out. They are gone for the rest of the battle (out of battle: they sit out the next one) and come back on 50 stress. |
| **Have It Out** | The party | They say all of it. They stay in the fight on 80 stress. Every other party member takes 20 stress, flat. |
| **Bottle It** | The character | They swallow it. They stay in the fight on 80 stress and take a Scar now: their Affliction's own, or Frayed Nerves if they have no Affliction or already carry that Scar. |

- All three leave the character under 100 with a clear head, so the next time they reach 100 they roll a fresh Resolve Check.
- An Affliction or Virtue that was rolled on doubles still leaves its mark when a Breakdown ends it. Bottling a doubles Affliction costs one Scar, not two.
- A held Silver Lining pays for a Bottle: the Lining and the Scar cancel.
- Bottle It is not offered to a character who already carries both their Affliction's Scar and Frayed Nerves.
- **A second Breakdown in the same battle is not a choice.** They Storm Off.

**The choice.** Lose a body for the rest of the fight, load 20 stress onto each of three people who may be near 100 themselves, or write something permanent on the sheet. Which is cheapest depends on the fight you are in.

### 3.9 Relief

Relief is flat. Willpower does not change it, level does not change it, and an Affliction can (Doomsayer halves it; a Tinfoil Helm in a mood refuses it from allies).

**In battle**

| Way | Stress removed | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **Breather** (anyone, any time) | 8, yourself only | Your whole turn. It is deliberately weaker than anything you pay for. |
| A comfort skill or item | about 15, one ally | MP or the item, and the user's turn. Guide value for content. |
| A rally skill | about 5, every ally | MP and the turn. Guide value for content. |
| Landing a critical hit | 3, yourself | Nothing. |
| A snap-out cure (Smelling Salts) | Ends the Affliction now. Stress above 90 drops to 90. | The item and a turn, and a gamble: the next bad moment is a new Resolve Check, with a new bill for the party if it goes badly. |
| A Virtue starting | 40 or 50 for its owner, 5–12 for everyone else | Letting someone reach 100. |

**Between battles**

| Way | Stress removed | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| Winning a battle | 5 each, 15 each after a boss | Winning. |
| **Camp** | Each character picks one: **Sleep** (restore 50% of max HP and MP) or **Unwind** (30 stress). | One Camp Kit for the party, and the pick itself: the bars or the nerves, not both. |
| **Inn night** | 25, on top of what an inn does for HP and MP | 20 gold × level, per head. |
| **Night Off** (town) | Everything: stress to 0, and any Affliction ends. | 80 gold × level, per head. |
| **Talk It Out** (town) | None. Removes one Scar. | A major purchase: the price of a new weapon. |

| Service | Price rule | L1 | L10 | L25 | L50 | L75 | L100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Camp Kit (whole party, one camp) | 1 fight's gold | 40 | 400 | 1,000 | 2,000 | 3,000 | 4,000 |
| Inn night (per head) | 0.5 fight's gold | 20 | 200 | 500 | 1,000 | 1,500 | 2,000 |
| Night Off (per head) | 2 fights' gold | 80 | 800 | 2,000 | 4,000 | 6,000 | 8,000 |
| Talk It Out (per Scar) | a major purchase | 160 | 2,500 | 10,000 | 32,500 | 67,500 | 115,000 |
| *For scale: gold from one fight* |  | *40* | *400* | *1,000* | *2,000* | *3,000* | *4,000* |

The inn is cheap and does not fix nerves: a night's sleep takes 25 off a bar of 200. Stress is the attrition that follows the party from fight to fight until somebody pays for a Night Off.

**The choice.** At camp, Sleep or Unwind. In town, a Night Off for a party of four costs eight fights' worth of gold. In battle, a Breather is always there and always worse than attacking.

### 3.10 Enemies and stress

Enemies have a stress bar and no Resolve table. Heroes' stress skills fill it, mitigated by the enemy's WIL like any other stress. The two rungs are the statuses `rattled` and `routed` in `data/skills.json`; `enemyStressEvent` applies them.

| Enemy stress | Status | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| reaching 100 | **Rattled** | Until the battle ends its attacks get −10 to hit and it fumbles on 91 or more. Both are band-edge moves on its own attack roll. |
| reaching 200 | **Routed** | A minion or standard enemy runs: it is defeated and pays full rewards. An elite, boss or superboss loses its next action, drops back to 100 stress and stays Rattled. It can be Routed again. |

**The choice.** A stress skill does no damage. Against a crowd it is a second way to empty the field; against a boss it buys lost actions and a wider fumble band at the price of turns you did not spend on its HP.

### 3.11 Body-part binds

Three parts can be bound: head, arms and legs. A bound part does two things for as long as the bind lasts.

| Part | Halves (once, section 1.1) | Seals |
|---|---|---|
| **Head** | Arcana and Willpower | Skills with `part: head`: spells, shouts, songs, bites, breath, and most stress skills |
| **Arms** | Might | Skills with `part: arms`: weapon techniques, shields, throws |
| **Legs** | Finesse | Skills with `part: legs`: kicks, charges, footwork. Running from the battle. Standing in front of an ally (cover). |

- **The basic attack is never sealed.** It is an arms skill, bound arms halve its Might, and it still works. Skills with `part: none`, items, Guard, the Breather and Struggle are never sealed either.
- **Binds never touch Max HP or Max MP.** They change what an attribute does this turn.
- Two binds never quarter anything: each attribute is governed by one part, and a second bind on a bound part refreshes it.

**The chance to bind.** Luck against Luck, the same bounded contest every non-attack check uses, scaled by the target's resistance:

```
Swing  = 60 × (binder LCK − target LCK) ÷ (binder LCK + target LCK)         both effective; no binder (a trap), no swing
Bind % = clamp( (skill chance + Swing + bonus) × (1 − bind resist ÷ 100) × 0.5 ^ earlier binds , 5 , 95 )
```

- **Skill chance** is the `chance` on the skill's bind effect.
- **Bind resist** is the target's `bindResist` for that part, 0–80, a percentage off the chance. Heroes have 0 unless gear says otherwise. Bosses carry it.
- **Earlier binds** is how many contested binds have already landed on that part of that combatant this battle.
- **bonus** is flat points from a passive or an item, if any.
- Multiply everything, round once at the end, a half rounds up, then clamp. The bracket is never less than 0.

Even Luck gives the chance printed on the skill. Double Luck is +20, half is −20, and no amount of Luck gets past 95 or under 5.

| Binder Luck ÷ target Luck | Swing | Base 25 | Base 35 | Base 45 | Base 55 | Base 80 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.25 | −36 | 5 | 5 | 9 | 19 | 44 |
| 0.5 | −20 | 5 | 15 | 25 | 35 | 60 |
| 0.67 | −12 | 13 | 23 | 33 | 43 | 68 |
| **1 (even)** | 0 | 25 | 35 | 45 | 55 | 80 |
| 1.5 | +12 | 37 | 47 | 57 | 67 | 92 |
| 2 | +20 | 45 | 55 | 65 | 75 | 95 |
| 4 | +36 | 61 | 71 | 81 | 91 | 95 |

**Reading the roll.** One d100, low is good. For a 55% bind: 1–5 crit, 6–55 takes, 56–95 fails, 96–100 fumble.

1. **Fumble**: 96 or more. Always a failure, even at 95%. **Fumble a bind and you wear it:** the binder's own same part is bound for 1 turn.
2. **Crit**: at or under a tenth of the chance (never less than a roll of 1). The bind lasts one turn longer.
3. **Takes**: at or under the chance.
4. **Fails**: anything else.

**A bind riding on an attack is read off the attack's own roll.** An attack that also binds does not roll again: the bind takes if the attack landed and the attack's d100 is at or under the bind chance. A skill that says a crit all but guarantees the bind passes the higher chance when the attack roll was a crit. Only a pure bind skill rolls a d100 of its own, and only that roll can backfire.

**Accumulating resistance.** Every contested bind that lands halves the chance of the next one on that part of that combatant, for the rest of the battle. A 55% bind goes 55, then 28, then 14, then 7. Only that part wears. Ticking out, being cured and Struggling free do not give the chance back; the end of the battle does. Accidents do not count: a befuddled fumble, a backfire, a skill that binds its own user and an event's bind are applied with no contest and build no resistance.

| Bind resist | Base 45: 1st / 2nd / 3rd / 4th bind | Base 55: 1st / 2nd / 3rd / 4th bind | Base 80: 1st / 2nd / 3rd / 4th bind |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 45 / 23 / 11 / 6 | 55 / 28 / 14 / 7 | 80 / 40 / 20 / 10 |
| 10 | 41 / 20 / 10 / 5 | 50 / 25 / 12 / 6 | 72 / 36 / 18 / 9 |
| 20 | 36 / 18 / 9 / 5 | 44 / 22 / 11 / 6 | 64 / 32 / 16 / 8 |
| 30 | 32 / 16 / 8 / 5 | 39 / 19 / 10 / 5 | 56 / 28 / 14 / 7 |
| 40 | 27 / 14 / 7 / 5 | 33 / 17 / 8 / 5 | 48 / 24 / 12 / 6 |
| 50 | 23 / 11 / 6 / 5 | 28 / 14 / 7 / 5 | 40 / 20 / 10 / 5 |
| 60 | 18 / 9 / 5 / 5 | 22 / 11 / 6 / 5 | 32 / 16 / 8 / 5 |
| 70 | 14 / 7 / 5 / 5 | 17 / 8 / 5 / 5 | 24 / 12 / 6 / 5 |
| 80 | 9 / 5 / 5 / 5 | 11 / 6 / 5 / 5 | 16 / 8 / 5 / 5 |

**Duration.**

- A bind lasts the number of turns on the skill (`turns`, 2 if it does not say), capped at 5.
- Turns are the bound combatant's own. Cross one off every bind at the end of each of its turns. A bind it picked up **during** its own turn (a befuddled fumble, a backfire) is not crossed off at the end of that turn, so a 1-turn self-bind still costs the next turn.
- **Refresh, never stack.** A new bind on a bound part sets it to the longer of the two durations. Refreshing early wastes the turns left and still spends a step of resistance.

**Trussed.** Head, arms and legs all bound at once. A Trussed target can use only its basic attack, part-less skills and items, and every hit on it is ×1.5 damage (`trussedDamageMult`). It lasts until the first of the three binds runs out.

**Cures.**

| Cure | What it does | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| Time | Each bind runs out on its own. | The turns. |
| A cure skill or item (`cureBinds`) | Removes every bind, or the parts it names. | MP or the item, and a turn. |
| **Struggle** (any hero, any time) | Frees one part of your choice. No roll. | Your whole turn. Bosses never Struggle; other enemies only if their data says so. |
| Second Wind's flourish | Shakes off every bind. | Nothing: it is part of the Virtue. |
| The battle ending | Every bind comes off and the resistance is forgotten. | |

**What each bind is worth.** Even attributes on both sides, average per action with misses and crits counted (`expectedDamage`).

| Bound | Their offence | Your offence against them | And |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Arms** | Physical damage ×0.375: 37.5% per action | unchanged | Weapon skills sealed. |
| **Head** | Spell damage ×0.375: 37.5%. Healing 50%. | Your spells do 120%. Stress on them lands a third heavier: 30 base at WIL 600 goes from 15 to 20. | Spells, shouts and most stress skills sealed. |
| **Legs** | To hit 75 → 67: 89.7% per action | You hit at 83: 110.3% per action | Acts after anyone with more than 60% of its Finesse. Cannot run, cannot cover. |
| **Trussed** | 33.6% with the basic attack, which is all it has | 165.5% with a blade, 198.6% with a spell | For as long as all three hold. |

**Worked example.** A level 50 Rogue (LCK 384) uses a 45% arms bind on an elite with LCK 240 and 30 arms resist. Swing 60 × (384 − 240) ÷ (384 + 240) = +13.8, so 45 becomes 58.8; × 0.7 for the resist is **41%**. The roll is 31: it takes, with no crit (that needed 4 or less), for 2 of the elite's turns. Its Might drops from 330 to 165, its arms skills are sealed and its basic attack does 40% of what it did. The next arms bind on it this battle is 21%.

At level 40, with LCK 325 against a benchmark 242, Valerie's Accidental Trip (55% on the card) is 64% on an unresisting enemy and 32% on a boss with 50 legs resist.

**The choice.** The first bind on each part is the cheap one, and there are only three firsts. Spend a 55% arms bind now and the big swing three rounds from now meets a 28% chance instead of a 55% one. Each part asks its own question:

- **Arms** is the largest single cut to incoming damage in the game, and it does nothing to a caster.
- **Head** silences a caster and softens it for your own Mage. On your side of the field a bound head is a stress problem: Willpower is halved.
- **Legs** prevents almost nothing. You are buying accuracy and the first move for everyone else.
- **Trussing** spends all three cheap chances at once for one burst window, instead of holding each as an answer.

### 3.12 At the table: quick reference

Everything above, as steps a game master can run by hand. If the game master can run code, call the functions in 3.13 and use this to explain the result.

**A. A stress hit.**
1. From the world: take the base on the move, or the tier from 3.3 at the party's level. Reduce it by the target's WIL with the table in section 1.8. From the party: use the number as written.
2. Apply any ×: Spooked and horror, Tinfoil Helm and mental, Martyr and wound, Unflappable and everything, a Scar.
3. Add it to the bar, 0–200.
4. Reached 200: Breakdown (D). Otherwise reached 100 from below with no Affliction or Virtue: Resolve Check (B).

**B. The Resolve Check.**
1. Shift: +5 per ally in a Virtue, −5 per Afflicted ally, +10 for Unflappable and −10 for Doomsayer instead, +5 or +10 if Valerie is Ditzy or a Total Airhead and it is not her check. No more than ±15.
2. Roll d100 and find it in that shift's column of the table in 3.4.
3. Even roll and the character has their own entry for that side: use theirs.
4. Doubles: note "permanent". It leaves a Scar (Affliction) or a Silver Lining (Virtue) when it ends.
5. Affliction: apply its attribute changes; every other party member takes its stress. Virtue: its owner sheds 40 or 50; every other party member sheds its number.

**C. The turn check** (start of an afflicted or virtuous character's turn).
1. Affliction: chance = base (30, 35 or 40) + 5 per full 20 stress over 100. Roll d100: up to 100 − chance composed, above that it acts out, 96–100 meltdown.
2. Virtue: roll d100, 30 or less is the flourish.
3. "Read off the dice" means roll modulo the number of candidates, counting from the top of the list.

**D. Breakdown** at 200. The player picks: Storm Off (gone for the battle, back on 50), Have It Out (stays on 80, every ally +20), Bottle It (stays on 80, takes a Scar). Second one in the same battle: Storm Off.

**E. Ending things.** Affliction: under 50 stress, a Night Off, Smelling Salts (stress to 90), a Breakdown. Virtue: the end of the battle, a Breakdown. Doubles: write the mark on the sheet now. At most 3 Scars and 1 Silver Lining.

**F. A bind.**
1. Start from the chance on the skill. Add the Luck swing: +20 at double the target's Luck, +12 at one and a half, 0 when even, −12 at two thirds, −20 at half.
2. Take off the target's bind resist as a percentage. Halve once for every bind that has already landed on that part this battle. Keep it within 5–95.
3. Roll d100 (or read the attack's roll if the bind rides on an attack). 96+ fails, and a pure bind skill backfires on the binder for 1 turn. A tenth of the chance or less: one extra turn. At or under the chance: bound.
4. Bound: halve the part's attributes, seal its skills (never the basic attack), cross off one turn at the end of each of the target's own turns. All three parts: ×1.5 damage taken.

## 4. MASTER ASSET REGISTRY (SPELLS, PASSIVES, ITEMS)

This section lists everything a party member can learn or buy: 54 class skills, 22 passives, 27 statuses, 42 shop items and 10 gear tiers. The data is `data/skills.json`, `data/passives.json` and `data/items.json`. `test/content.test.js` pins the rules on this page. A script printed every table below from that data. The damage, healing, stress and bind figures come from running it through `engine/combat.js`, `engine/progression.js` and `engine/binds.js`.

The hero's own class, the Klutz Knight, is in section 6. Boss and enemy moves are in section 5.

One rule shaped the whole registry: costs are flat. A skill learned at level 1 costs 1 MP forever. By level 10 that is less than half a percent of anyone's bar. MP is therefore only the price of a *new* skill.

Every skill needs a second price that never wears off: a missed turn, a body part, stress, or exposure. Read each entry for that second price.

### 4.1 How to read a skill entry

```json
{
  "id": "war_cleave", "name": "Cleave", "archetype": "warrior", "unlock": 5, "tier": 1,
  "type": "physical", "stat": "STR", "power": 3, "acc": 0, "crit": 0,
  "element": "phys", "target": "row", "part": "arms",
  "cost": { "mp": 12, "limit": 0 }, "mpShare": 0.07,
  "tags": ["melee"],
  "effects": [{ "kind": "bind", "part": "legs", "chance": 40, "turns": 2 }],
  "text": "A wide, low swing through one row. 40% base chance to bind each target's legs for 2 turns."
}
```

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `unlock`, `tier` | The level the skill is learned at. Tier is its ten-level bracket: tier 1 is levels 1–10, tier 10 is levels 91–100. |
| `type` | `physical` and `magical` deal damage. `heal` restores HP. `buff`, `debuff`, `stress` and `utility` deal none. |
| `stat`, `power` | The attribute the skill attacks or heals with, and its Power. Each point of Power is 5% of the bar of a peer whose defense equals that attribute (section 1.3). Constitution defends against a physical skill whatever it attacks with. Willpower defends against a magical one. |
| `acc`, `crit` | Added to the chance to hit and the chance to crit. Accuracy runs −20 to +20, crit 0 to +20. |
| `element` | `phys`, `fire`, `ice`, `bolt`, `holy` or `dark`. The target's affinity multiplies the damage. |
| `target` | `one`, `row` or `all` aim at enemies and roll one d100 for each enemy. `self`, `ally` and `allies` aim at the party and roll nothing. |
| `part` | `head`, `arms`, `legs` or `none`. A bound part seals its skills. No bind seals `none`. |
| `cost` | Flat MP, or Limit for an ultimate. It stays the same from the day the skill is learned. |
| `mpShare` | The share of `mpBudget(unlock)` that set the MP cost. |
| `tags` | `melee`, `ranged`, `projectile`: how the attack travels. `mental`, `embarrassment`: the stress source it carries. `ultimate`: spends Limit. `scripted`: the text holds a rule the `effects` list cannot express. |
| `effects` | What else happens. See the table below. |
| `text` | The exact rule in one line, as the player and the game master read it. |

**The cost rule.**

```
cost.mp = mpShare × mpBudget(unlock level), rounded to two significant figures, at least 1
```

`mpBudget(level)` is the MP bar of a character with benchmark Willpower: 640 at level 10, 15,100 at level 50, 60,000 at level 100. The three bars differ in size: the warrior holds half the budget, the rogue two thirds, the mage four thirds. Each archetype therefore has its own share ladder. A skill of a given weight empties any bar at about the same pace. The mage gets a third more casts, because casting is all the mage does.

| Weight | Warrior share | Rogue share | Mage share | Casts from a full bar when it unlocks (W / R / M, at level 50) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| light | 3% | 4% | 6% | 17 / 16 / 22 |
| standard | 5% | 6% | 10% | 10 / 11 / 13 |
| heavy | 7% | 9% | 14% | 6 / 7 / 9 |
| signature | 10% | 13% | 20% | 5 / 5 / 6 |

- **Light:** a stance, a setup or a small utility.
- **Standard:** a bread-and-butter attack or tool.
- **Heavy:** an area attack, a big hit or a strong tool.
- **Signature:** the show-stopper of its tier.

A full bar pays for every skill at least four times at the level the skill unlocks. Ultimates cost no MP: each archetype has one at 50 or 60 Limit and one that takes the whole gauge of 100.

**Effects.**

| `kind` | Fields | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `bind` | `part`, `chance`, `turns` | Tries to bind that part. `chance` is the base chance. `engine/binds.js` adds a Luck contest, takes off the target's bind resistance, and halves the chance for every earlier bind on that part this battle. A bind riding on an attack reads the attack's own d100. |
| `stress` | `amount`, `source`, optional `ignoreWill` | A positive amount adds stress. The target's Willpower reduces it unless `ignoreWill` is set. A negative amount is relief, and nothing reduces relief. `source` is one of general, horror, mental, realization, embarrassment, wound, death. |
| `mod` | `stat`, `mult`, `turns` | Multiplies one attribute for that many turns. The multipliers on one attribute combine, and the engine holds the product to ×0.5–×1.5. |
| `status` | `id`, `turns` | Applies a status from the registry in 4.6. |
| `dot` | `power`, `turns`, `element` | Damage over time. At the start of each of the target's next turns it takes a hit of that Power and element from the caster: no roll, no crit, no variance. Affinity applies. |
| `cleanse` | `what`: `binds`, `mods` or `statuses` | On an ally: removes binds, debuffs or harmful statuses. On an enemy: removes its buffs or helpful statuses. |
| `heal` | `pct`, optional `cap`, optional `bar: "mp"` | Items only. Restores that share of the max bar, up to the ceiling. A negative share drains. |
| `revive` | `pct` | Brings a downed ally back at that share of max HP. |
| `limit` | `amount` | Adds to the Limit gauge. |

**How an action resolves.**

1. Skills are battle actions. Outside battle, HP and MP come back from items, camp and inns.
2. Pay the cost. A character cannot choose a skill whose part is bound.
3. An effect marked `"target": "self"` is the price or the stance. It lands on the user once per use, hit or miss, unless it also says `"onHit": true`. A self-bind uses `applyBind` and no roll, which is why its `chance` reads 100.
4. A skill aimed at enemies rolls one d100 for each enemy, with `acc` and `crit`. On a miss or a fumble nothing else in the skill touches that enemy.
5. On a hit or a crit the enemy takes the damage, then every unmarked effect. Skills with Power 0 (`debuff`, `stress`) still roll to hit and deal no damage.
6. A skill aimed at the party rolls nothing. Healing is `healAmount` for each target, then every unmarked effect lands on each target.
7. Durations count the bearer's own turns and tick at the end of each, as binds do. Something a character puts on itself during its own turn starts counting on its next turn. Guard is the one exception: its entry in 4.6 says when it ends.

Rules text uses the attribute names the player sees: Might (STR), Arcana (INT), Finesse (AGI), Constitution (CON), Willpower (WIL), Luck (LCK).

**Scripted skills.** 8 skills carry the `scripted` tag: Grudge Match, Closing Time, Group Project, Dissertation, Clean Getaway, Borrowed Time, Punchline, The Big Score. Each one's text states a rule that needs code of its own.

### 4.3 Mage: 16 skills and 2 ultimates

- **Want:** To understand the thing, name the thing and set the thing on fire, in that order.
- **Cost:** Everything below the neck is on loan: one good shove and the lecture is over.

**How the tree says it.** The want is the order of operations.

- **Understand.** The level 1 passive Marginalia reveals an affinity the first time each element lands, and pays Limit for it.
- **Name.** Cold Feet, Closed Book and Peer Review bind, silence and strip.
- **Set on fire.** Five elements, and no two spells the same shape:
  - Fire burns over time.
  - Ice pins legs and slows.
  - Bolt gambles on crits.
  - Dark attacks the stress bar.

### 4.4 Rogue: 16 skills and 2 ultimates

- **Want:** To get in, get the prize and get out before anyone finishes saying "hey".

#### What stress does to an enemy

Enemies carry the same 0–200 stress bar as the party, and their Willpower reduces what they take by the same formula (section 1.6). They roll no Resolve Check. `enemyStressEvent` in `engine/stress.js` gives them two rungs instead:

| Status | id | Kind | Rule |

### 4.7 Economy Item Matrix

Prices come from the two helpers in section 2.6, rounded to two significant figures.

- **Consumables** cost a number of reference fights: `priceForFights(level, fights)`. One fight pays 40 gold at level 1, 400 at level 10, 2,000 at level 50 and 4,000 at level 100.
- **Seeds and gear** cost a share of a level's gold: `priceAt(level, share)`.
- **Level** is the level an item is sized and priced for. A lower-level party with the gold can still buy it.
- The party starts with 160 gold.

Gold income grows with the square of the level and prices stay fixed. An item that restores a share of a bar therefore needs a ceiling, or it would be free by mid-game. Stress does not scale with level, so gold alone cannot buy relief: each calming item also takes MP or a turn, or works once per rest.

### 5.2 Rules every boss follows

- **One question per phase.** Each phase changes one thing and asks the party one thing. Every phase in the data carries `question`, `answer` and `price`: what is being asked, a way through, and what that way costs.
- **A want and a cost.** Each boss has an `identity`, a `want` and a `cost`, and a **concession**: in the last phase the party can give the boss what it wants and end the fight as a win. The concession is never free, and it is always paid in something Valerie needs.
- **Built for the hero.** Every boss has a projectile attack from phase 1 (Kinetic Deflection), fear from phase 1 (Inspirational Ignorance: she ignores it, the party does not), a realization move by phase 2 that cannot miss (the truth is the one thing she cannot shrug off), and an arena contraption that an Accidental Chaos Wave sets off.
- **Winging it or thinking it through.** Bosses are accurate, so Unintentional Dodge is worth the most against them, and each one has a mechanic that only planning answers reliably. The player is meant to want both ends of the gauge in the same fight.
- **Stress is a second health bar.** Each boss has at least three stress moves from at least three sources. Stress amounts are written flat, because stress does not scale with level; Willpower does the scaling.
- **Nothing unannounced kills.** A single-target move tops out at Power 8 and a row or all-target move at Power 4, and no such move can drop any reference hero from full HP even on a crit at maximum variance. The one move per boss that can is telegraphed at least a round ahead, can be cancelled at least two ways, and never kills a hero who Guards from full HP.

Things the data says beyond the FOUNDATION Skill shape:

| Field | Where | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `actions` | boss, enemy | Actions per round. A boss acts this many times; the round scripts list them in order. |
| `script` | phase | A repeating cycle of rounds, each a list of move ids. It is the boss's default behaviour and the thing the simulation measures. |
| `question`, `answer`, `price` | phase | The choice the phase forces, for the designer and the game master. |
| `auto: true` | move | No attack roll. The move cannot miss, be dodged, bounce or fumble. Speech and roars work this way. |

How the simulation reads a fight:

- **The party** is the Warrior, Mage and Rogue from `data/archetypes.json` and the Klutz Knight from `data/valerie.json`, at the boss's level, with no gear and full bars.
- **Party offense** is `expectedDamage` per action. "Basic" is the Power 2 basic attack from everyone. "Power 4" is a typical Power 4 skill: Might for the Warrior, Rogue and Valerie, an Arcana spell at neutral affinity for the Mage.
- **Valerie** Wings It at Airhead 60, her starting gauge, unless a table says otherwise. Enemy attacks on her go through `incomingOpts` and `readIncoming`: the lowered hit ceiling, the bounce band and the widened fumble band.
- **Boss offense** averages each phase's script over its cycle. An aimed attack is spread evenly (a quarter each), a row attack catches the front row (Warrior, Valerie) or the back row (Mage, Rogue) half the time each, and an all-target move rolls against everyone. All 100 faces of the d100 are counted, so these are exact expectations, not samples.
- **Telegraphed moves** are left out of the per-round figures and shown on their own line, as if nobody answered them.
- **Phases** are weighted by their share of the HP bar.
- **Stress** is `stressTaken` for the allies and `stressOnValerie` for the hero, with the 3 points allies shed whenever she shrugs off fear. It counts stress from boss moves only. The standard sources in `engine/stress.js` (a crit taken, an ally down, low HP) come on top; a phase change is not treated as a separate stress hit, because each phase's stress is already written into the moves it adds.

#### Contraption and concession

**The Great Wheel** (once per phase). A wheel of fortune three storeys tall, chained since the day she took office because a wheel that spins is an unaudited liability. When a Chaos Wave sets it off the chain snaps and it spins: Madam Parity is Surprised for the first time in her existence and loses her next action, she takes damage equal to 2% of her max HP, a flipped ledger flips back, and a rung Closing Bell is cancelled. Works once per phase.

**One for the House** (from 25% HP). At 25% HP or below, a Valerie who is truly a Total Airhead (gauge 67 or more, ledger not flipped) can spend 100 MCE to give the Wheel's next spin to Madam Parity. It lands on her number. She has never won anything before, and the fight ends as a win with full rewards. *The price:* All 100 MCE, saved instead of spent on either ultimate, and Valerie has to be an Airhead at the moment it matters, which the Itemised Statement is working against.

#### Tactics, counterplay, rewards

- **Tactics.** Three actions a round. Every single-target attack goes to the hero with the highest current HP; a taunt overrides that. Double Entry comes round on a four-round cycle from Phase 2, Repossession from Phase 3 and the Closing Bell from Phase 4. Her Luck is exactly the benchmark for her level, so she neither out-lucks anyone nor is out-lucked.
- **Counterplay.** Make Valerie the richest target in the room and let the coins bounce; dark hurts Parity and holy is halved. When the ledger flips, either Think through it and accept the stress or spend 100 MCE on I'm Just a Good Hero! to ignore it, and time buffs for the round after a Repossession. When the bell rings, spin the Great Wheel with a Chaos Wave, taunt so Valerie takes the bill alone, or have everyone Guard.
- **Against the hero's kit.** Madam Parity audits the kit itself: she aims by HP so the dodge has to be earned, turns the gauge upside down so Thinking is lucky, reads out the receipt for every accident, and can only be ended early by the one thing she has never had, a stroke of luck.
- **Rewards.** 500,000 EXP to each party member and 100,000 gold: `expYield(100, 'superboss')` and `goldYield(100, 'superboss')`. No item ids are named yet.

### 5.7 What these designs assume the engine will do

The engine resolves one action at a time. The bosses assume a battle loop around it that does not exist yet. Each line is something the Director must wire up.

1. **Several actions a round.** `actions` on a boss or enemy, taken in script order.
2. **Phase changes and scripts.** Switch phase when HP crosses `at`, restart the script at round 1, and re-cock a once-per-phase contraption.
3. **Wind-up and telegraph.** A `windup` move sets a countdown; the telegraphed move fires as the first action `telegraph.rounds` later unless its part is bound, the contraption goes off or the party concedes.
4. **Cannot-miss moves.** `auto: true` skips the attack roll, so there is nothing for Unintentional Dodge, the bounce or the Aura to act on.
5. **Effect targets** `valerie`, `others`, `heroes`, and the `orPay` choice on a stress effect.
6. **Contraptions.** `chaosWave()` already returns a `contraption` effect aimed at the field; the loop has to look up the arena's contraption and apply its `effects`, including `damage` by `hpPct` and binds that ignore bind resist. The `manual` hard check is the Think It Through way to do the same.
7. **Guard as a status on an enemy,** stripped by a Chaos Wave (`guard_broken`) and, for Vellum, by Wait, Look Closely.
8. **Concessions.** A story action that ends the fight as a win and charges its `effects`.
9. **Ledger Flipped.** While the status is on Valerie, `passiveAirhead()` must return 100 minus the gauge; I'm Just a Good Hero! still pins it at 100.
10. **Madam Parity's targeting rule** (highest current HP first), **Repossession** (move every hero buff to the boss) and **Balance Due's redirect** onto a taunting Valerie.
11. **Crowd limits.** `acting` on an enemy caps how many of that kind act each round.
12. **Bosses do not Struggle.** A bind that lands on a boss runs its full turns; bind resist and the wear rule in `engine/binds.js` are the price of landing one.
13. **Taunts and speech.** A taunt redirects attacks. It does not redirect a move whose effects name their own targets (the readings, the bellow).

## 6. THE HERO: VALERIE

**Dame Valerie Bellweather**, Klutz Knight. You were an ordinary person in an ordinary world. You woke up in a forest wearing golden armor, holding a sword taller than you are, and everyone keeps calling you Dame Valerie. The body is hers. The thoughts are yours. Her luck only works when you stop steering.

Long pink hair, bright green eyes, a permanent look of mild surprise. Gleaming gold plate that was clearly designed by an optimist: pauldrons, gauntlets and thigh-high greaves over a deep teal cloak. Tall, soft and very curvy, built top-heavy enough that her center of gravity is a running joke the physics engine is in on.

### 6.3 The Airhead gauge

One gauge, 0 to 100, starting at 60. It is the whole character in one number: who is driving.

| Band | Gauge | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Lucid** | 0–33 | You are driving. Planned actions are sharp. Her lucky accidents barely happen, and everything scary is exactly as scary as it looks. |
| **Ditzy** | 34–66 | You share the wheel. Everything works a bit. The safe middle, and the least exciting place to be. |
| **Total Airhead** | 67–100 | The body is driving. Blows miss, arrows bounce, enemies fall over, and fear does not register. Planned skills are sealed: the thought just leaves. |

Every action is taken in a stance:

- **Wing It.** Act on instinct. Gauge +10, stress −2. Your fumble band widens with the gauge (up to 76–100), and a fumble becomes an Accidental Chaos Wave instead of a wasted turn.
- **Think It Through.** Use your own head. Gauge −15, +4 stress that Willpower cannot reduce. +15 to hit, +5 to crit, normal fumble band. Required for planned skills.

**The choice.** Lucky or precise, never both. Winging it makes her nearly untouchable and takes aimed damage away from you. Thinking gives you your skills back and strips the luck that was keeping her alive.

### 6.4 The six accidents

None of these adds a roll. Each one moves a band edge on a d100 that was going to be rolled anyway. All six are innate from level 1. They are who she is, not rewards: staggering them would make the opening hours play like a different character. Her growth comes from the skill tree and from learning to ride the gauge.

#### Unintentional Dodge

*A monster swings; she trips and falls under the blow. She believes she is being agile.*

- **Rule.** Enemies attacking Valerie have their hit ceiling lowered from 95 to 95 − 0.4 × Airhead (55 at full gauge). Guarding on purpose switches it off for the turn.
- **The choice.** Guard halves damage you take. Not guarding lets the dodge work. Pick one each turn.
- **Engine.** `incomingOpts().hitCap` in `engine/valerie.js`

#### Pocket Dimension

*The lunch, the map, the one key that matters. It is in the cloak. It was always in the cloak. She is just really good at finding things.*

- **Rule.** After each battle, one consumable may go Missing (10% + Airhead/4). Missing items are not lost: once per battle or scene, when an ally drops below 25% HP or a scene calls for an item, roll her Luck against a normal check (+Airhead/5) to have a fitting Missing item turn up and be used for free. Slots: 3 + 1 per 20 levels. Once per rest you may Misplace an item on purpose.
- **The choice.** An item in your pack is yours to use when you decide. An item in the cloak costs no turn and arrives at the perfect moment, if it arrives.
- **Engine.** `misplacePct(), foundPct(), pocketSlots()` in `engine/valerie.js`

#### Kinetic Deflection (The Booby Bounce)

*Arrows, rocks and magic missiles meet a chest that defies physics, stop, reconsider, and leave the way they came. She thinks she bounced it off. She is not wrong.*

- **Rule.** Against attacks tagged projectile, the top of the attacker's hit band becomes a bounce band: Airhead/4 points wide, plus up to 10 for out-lucking the shooter. A roll in that band deals no damage to her and sends the shot back at the attacker for the damage it would have done, resolved against the attacker's own defense. The crit band is never eaten.
- **The choice.** It only answers projectiles, and only while the gauge is high. Standing in the open to bait archers means standing in the open.
- **Engine.** `incomingOpts().bounce, readIncoming()` in `engine/valerie.js`

#### Accidental Chaos Wave

*She trips, lands hard, and the floor disagrees with everyone else. Her sword clips a wall and something expensive starts happening.*

- **Rule.** When Valerie fumbles while Winging It, the fumble is a Chaos Wave: every enemy is Off-Balance (Finesse ×0.75 for 2 turns) and loses its Guard, she gains 15 Limit, and any contraption on the field goes off. On a natural 100 the wave also hits every enemy for Power 4 physical damage. A fumble while Thinking is just a miss.
- **The choice.** Up to a quarter of your Wing It attacks become waves instead of damage. Wonderful against a crowd, a wasted turn against one big target.
- **Engine.** `stanceEffects('wing').opts.fumbleFrom, chaosWave()` in `engine/valerie.js`

#### Aura of Befuddlement

*They drop weapons, trip on nothing, forget which end of the spear is the business end. Nobody connects it to her. She certainly does not.*

- **Rule.** Enemies attacking Valerie fumble on 96 − Airhead/10 or higher (86+ at full gauge). An enemy that fumbles this way binds one of its own body parts for 1 turn, chosen by the roll.
- **The choice.** It only reaches enemies who are attacking her. To use it you have to be the one they are swinging at.
- **Engine.** `incomingOpts().fumbleFrom, befuddledPart()` in `engine/valerie.js`

#### Inspirational Ignorance

*She faces the lich with the calm of someone who has not understood the situation. The party mistakes it for courage and finds some of their own.*

- **Rule.** While not Lucid, Valerie takes no stress from horror or mental sources, and every ally who sees it sheds 3 stress. Allies making a Resolve Check gain +5 Virtue chance while she is Ditzy, +10 while she is a Total Airhead. The price: realization stress ignores her Willpower and drops the gauge by 10; embarrassment stress is ×1.5 unless she is too far gone to notice.
- **The choice.** The party's nerve rests on her not understanding. Anyone who explains what really happened, including you, is spending it.
- **Engine.** `stressFilter(), resolveShiftForAllies()` in `engine/valerie.js`

### 6.6 Her Resolve Check

Valerie rolls the same 25/75 check as everyone at 100 stress, on the same single d100. When that roll is an even number, the table result is replaced by one of her own:

- **Virtue: A Good Hero.** She decides the evidence is on her side: stress stops reaching her at all, and about one turn in three every ally sheds 5. She sheds 40 stress and allies shed 10 when it starts.
